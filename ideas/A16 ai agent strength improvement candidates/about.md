# A16+ — Candidates for beating `A11`'s rating-74 ceiling

**Status**: strategic notes only, captured 2026-09-09 in response to Jonathan's direct
question ("how do we improve on `ismctsnn`'s 74?"), asked right after `A14` registered
at 62 — well below `A11`, and the fourth "add a smart-sounding mechanism on top of an
already-good agent" attempt in this project to measure at parity or worse. This doc is
the strategic starting point for whichever of these gets built next — reserved as
`A16`, with `A17`/`A18`/... open if more than one is pursued. Not a committed design
for any single agent; a ranked set of candidate directions with the reasoning behind
the ranking, so a future session doesn't have to re-derive it from `git log`.

## The meta-pattern this ranking is built on

Looking across the whole ladder's real, measured results (not the design-intent
estimates, which ran optimistic almost everywhere):

**Changing *what gets learned* has a clean track record of real wins:**
- `A5`'s heuristic rollout policy fixed `A10`'s rollout-bias ceiling (a
  budget-vs-rating curve that plateaued at 46-48% vs `Borealis` regardless of search
  budget, fixed by swapping the rollout policy -- a ~15-20 point jump).
- `A10`'s rollout replaced by a trained value net for `A11` -- a genuine, well-powered
  win (58.44% head-to-head vs `A10`, +5 rating).

**Adding a smarter mechanism on top of an already-good agent has a clean track record
of failing, despite sound reasoning going in every time:**
- `A9`'s `reply_trust` (opponent-response ply) -- near-parity with `A7`, later found to
  decline monotonically with more trust in the mechanism.
- `A13`'s `hplus_trust` (Jensen-corrected expected block) -- conclusively harmful,
  pinned to 0.
- `A13`'s Layer R (race-aware `defense_stdev_mult`) and the other three closed-form
  layers -- parity with `A7`, never a strength gain, across four independent
  calibration searches.
- `A14`'s entire PUCT apparatus (learned prior + PUCT selection formula) -- parity with
  `A11` head-to-head, an estimated rating *twelve points below* `A11`'s own, and a
  dial-calibration effort (four sweeps + a properly-revalidated joint optimize pass,
  n=16,000 at the end) that found literally nothing across all four free dials.

**Takeaway used to rank the candidates below**: prioritize directions that change the
*signal quality* a model learns from over directions that add search/formula
sophistication on top of an already-good agent. The latter category has now struck out
four times in a row with sound reasoning every time; the former has a clean 2-for-2
record.

## Candidate 1 (top pick, reserved as `A16`) -- genuine multi-round self-play bootstrapping

Every trained net in this project so far -- `A11`'s value net, `A14`'s value+policy
net -- was trained on exactly **one round of imitation** from an existing agent
(`A11` imitates `A10`'s self-play; `A14` imitates `A11`'s self-play). No agent has
ever been retrained on data generated by *itself*. This is the one ingredient
AlphaZero-class engines actually derive most of their strength from, and this project
has designed for it (`PUCTParams.root_dirichlet_alpha`/`root_noise_frac` exist
specifically for exploration noise during self-play generation) but never executed it.

`A14`'s own diagnosis for why PUCT didn't beat plain UCT supports this directly: the
learned prior's ceiling is `A11` itself, because it only ever imitated `A11` once --
"the gain must come from spending the same iteration budget better, not a better
evaluator, since the prior cannot exceed what it imitates" (the plan's own Risks
section, written before any measurement).

**Why this wasn't already tried**: Stage 6 of `A14`'s plan explicitly designed a
self-play round 2 (bootstrap a new corpus from `A14`'s own play, retrain), but gated
it on the `prior_trust` sweep showing a genuine *rising* signature first (`A11`'s own
pattern) rather than a flat one (what `A14` actually measured). **Worth reconsidering
whether that gate was testing the right question**: "does trusting *this* prior help"
and "would a *stronger* prior, from bootstrapping, help" are different questions -- a
flat `prior_trust` sweep on round-1 data doesn't actually rule out round 2 paying off.

**What this would concretely involve**: play `A14` (or a successor) against itself
(and/or a curated opponent pool) at its shipped config, generate a new corpus from
*that* self-play (not `A11`'s), retrain the two-head net on it, re-measure. The
existing `aicalibsrc/puct/` tooling (`gen_policy_corpus.c`, `run_selfplay.sh`,
`train_puct_net.py`, `export_puct_weights.py`) needed a teacher *argument* (done
2026-09-22, `A16` Session 2 item 2 -- previously hardcoded to `ismctsnn`), and the
teacher is specifically `puct` running its own module defaults (`PUCT_DEFAULTS`,
`use_puct=false` as of that date -- i.e. today's real shipped `A14` config), not a
forced PUCT-selection variant -- "swap `ismctsnn` for `puct`" undersold what that
distinction actually requires. Most of Stage 2/3's infrastructure was otherwise
reusable as-is, confirmed by the actual generalization work.

**Open questions**: how many bootstrap rounds are worth running before diminishing
returns set in (unknown -- this project has no data on this yet, since round 1 is all
that's ever been tried); real exploration noise
(`root_dirichlet_alpha`/`root_noise_frac`) turned out to be declared but read by
nothing (confirmed by grep, 2026-09-22) -- not needed for round 1 regardless: plain
UCT's own root widening already structurally prevents the blind-spot failure mode
noise exists to fix (widening_cap=127 at 4000 visits, comfortably above ~93 typical
legal moves, so every legal root move gets expanded at least once by construction).
**Resolved, 2026-09-22**: the probe ran (item 2.4, 40 min, teacher=`puct` running
today's shipped config, `mirror,vs_a7,vs_a3` pool, `limit_iterations=4000`) --
40,093 records, 60,140 records/hour, **zero diversity collapse**: 0% duplicate
state vectors, 0% repeated (state, chosen-move) pairs among early-game records,
chosen-move entropy 2.87 of a possible 4.14 nats, essentially matching the
*existing* `A11`-taught corpus's own baseline (0% duplicates, 2.94/4.49 nats,
measured the same date for comparison). The structural argument above held up
empirically, not just on paper -- round 1 does not need round-2 exploration
noise. Label sanity also checked out (per-matchup outcomes directionally
consistent with known agent ratings: mirror 50.5%, `vs_a3` 70.1%, `vs_a7`
59.0%). **No blockers found for a real round-1 generation run.** Whether
round 2's corpus should still include the curated `vs_a7`/`vs_a3` opponents or
move toward pure self-play remains open, deferred to whenever a round 2 is
actually planned. See `doc/changelog.md`'s 2026-09-22 A16 Session 2 entry for
the full record.

## Candidate 2 (infrastructure, not necessarily its own agent) -- pthread-based root parallelization

Flagged repeatedly and never implemented (see `[[project_a10_a11_multicore_rollouts]]`
memory). Independent motivation from Candidate 1, but plausibly a **prerequisite for
Candidate 1 to pay off fully**: `A14`'s own diagnosis includes "PUCT's ~4x
per-decision cost may be eating the very budget the prior needs to pay off" -- more
effective iterations per wall-clock second at a fixed interactive latency budget
directly addresses that, independent of anything about self-play. Root
parallelization (independent trees per thread from the same root, merged via summed
visit counts at the end) is the natural fit for this project's existing IS-MCTS
structure and was already suggested as the approach, twice, in prior memory notes.

**2026-09-22 premise shift, flagged not resolved**: this "prerequisite" reasoning was
built on PUCT selection's own 1.7s/decision cost being what ships. That's no longer
true -- `A16` Session 1's item 1.1 measured `use_puct=false` (plain UCT selection
over `A14`'s own net) decisively beating `A11` head-to-head, and it shipped as `A14`'s
new default the same day (`doc/ai_agents.md`'s A14 section, 2026-09-22 addendum;
`doc/changelog.md`). Measured per-decision cost under the new default: **536ms mean**
(`bin/calib_puct_timing`, `limit_iterations=4000`), close to `A11`'s own 439ms --
nowhere near the 4x/1.7s problem this paragraph describes. PUCT selection itself
remains implemented and available via an explicit `use_puct=true` override, so the
original reasoning still applies to *that* configuration specifically, just not to
what a player gets by default anymore. Session 1's item 1.4 (gprof-then-cache
`compose_node_priors()`/`get_available_moves()`) was skipped as moot on this same
finding rather than executed against a now-mostly-dead code path (Jonathan's call,
2026-09-22). Whether Candidate 2 is still worth prioritizing as a Candidate-1
prerequisite, now that the cost driving that framing has largely evaporated, is an
open question for whoever picks up Session 4 -- not resolved here.

Not necessarily worth a new agent number -- more likely a shared infrastructure
improvement to `ai_strat_ismcts_search.c`/`ai_strat_puct_search.c` that benefits
`A10`/`A11`/`A14` (and any bootstrapped successor) alike, the same way the
`use_widening` ablation or the `nn_value_use_mover_seat` fix were structural additions
rather than new agents.

## Candidate 3 (speculative, lower confidence) -- engineered features as value-net inputs

`A13` Cartographer's four closed-form layers (race arithmetic, deck-aware draw
valuation, reshuffle-boundary awareness, Jensen-corrected expected block) never
improved on `A7` as a *deterministic* combination -- four independent calibration
searches all measured parity or worse. But that result is specifically about hand-
tuning fixed weights on top of a fixed heuristic; it says nothing about whether those
same derived quantities (turns-to-kill race numbers, live unseen-pool draw
expectations) would be *useful additional input features* to a value net that can
learn its own, potentially highly nonlinear and context-dependent, weighting of them
-- a genuinely different mechanism from a hand-tuned linear-ish blend. Untried in this
project. Would mean extending the 537-float state encoder (`ismctsnn_encode_state()`
or a successor) with a handful of `A13`-derived scalars, then retraining.

**Caveat driving the lower confidence ranking**: this is the closest of the four
candidates to "add sophistication on top of an already-good mechanism" -- the failure
category, not the success category -- just applied to feature engineering rather than
search formula design. Worth trying only after Candidate 1 is exhausted or found not
worth pursuing further, not before.

## Candidate 4 (speculative, exploits a real but unexplained finding) -- opponent-adaptive play

`A14`'s own real, well-powered finding: it ties `A11` head-to-head (49.34%, parity)
but measures a specifically worse win rate against `borealis` than `A11` does (61.80%
vs `A11`'s 74%) despite being roughly `A11`'s overall equal -- a genuine
non-transitive result between two different search mechanisms. This means aggregate
rating hides real, opponent-specific blind spots. No agent in this project currently
adapts its play based on any running estimate of "what kind of opponent is this" --
every agent (including the belief-modeling `A13`) reasons about hidden information,
never about the *opponent's playing style* specifically. An agent that tracked
something like the opponent's own defense-decline rate or attack-timing pattern over
the game so far, and adjusted its own risk-taking accordingly, would be a genuinely
new mechanism category for this roster, distinct from every prior agent's context-free
approach. Speculative and the least well-scoped of the four -- would need real design
work (what to track, how to adapt, how to avoid overfitting to noise early in a game)
before it's buildable, unlike Candidates 1-2 which are closer to "extend existing
tooling."

## Suggested sequencing if more than one is pursued

Candidate 1 first (highest confidence, matches the project's own 2-for-2 "signal
quality" success pattern, and the tooling mostly already exists). Candidate 2 either
alongside or just before it, since it plausibly gates how much Candidate 1 can actually
demonstrate within an interactive latency budget. Candidates 3 and 4 only after 1 (and
maybe 2) are tried and their results are in -- both are more speculative and, in
Candidate 3's case, structurally closer to the failure pattern this whole ranking is
built to avoid repeating.
